# Regulatory and Platform Compliance for MINMAX as a Global Wellness App (Not a Medical Device)

_Research report for MINMAX · 2026-10-02 · status: draft, independently fact-checked afterwards_

## Summary for the founder

- **The wellness/medical-device line is drawn by your words, not your sensors.** FDA (revised guidance, 6 Jan 2026), EU MDCG 2019-11 Rev.1 (June 2025) and Swissmedic (info sheet v3.0, April 2026) all say the same thing: a fitness app is not a medical device as long as its intended purpose contains no diagnosis, prevention, monitoring, prediction, prognosis or treatment of a disease. "Your VO2max is in the 80th percentile for your age" is fine; "your risk of heart disease is X" or "lower your LDL to 50" is a medical purpose [1][6][8] (confidence: high).
- **FDA 2026 is unusually generous for wearable-derived metrics.** Non-invasive estimates of HRV, SpO2, even blood pressure and glucose can be wellness outputs if there are no disease references, no diagnostic thresholds, no "abnormal/pathological" wording, and no prompts to clinical action. A "may help reduce the risk of" phrasing is allowed only for a short list of chronic diseases where the lifestyle link is settled (heart disease, high blood pressure, type 2 diabetes) [1] (confidence: high).
- **The EU is stricter on "prevention":** MDCG 2019-11 Rev.1 added an example that software intended to *prevent the risk of illness by analysing physiological parameters* can be class IIa. MINMAX should never frame its stats or Bottleneck as risk prevention for a disease [6] (confidence: high).
- **Health data = GDPR Art. 9 special category everywhere MINMAX operates.** Legal basis is explicit consent; a DPIA is mandatory (the German DPAs list health apps as always high-risk); Switzerland's revised FADP requires express consent for sensitive data and high-risk profiling [10][15] (confidence: high).
- **The EU AI Act is a light touch for MINMAX today:** a lifestyle recommendation engine is minimal-risk; only Art. 50 transparency (tell users when they talk to an AI) applies from 2 Aug 2026. The Digital Omnibus (Reg. (EU) 2026/1744) delayed Annex III high-risk duties to 2 Dec 2027. Danger zones: insurance-eligibility use cases (Annex III) and manipulative/dark-pattern gamification (Art. 5) [12][13][14] (confidence: high).
- **US: no HIPAA, but a thick patchwork.** Washington's My Health My Data Act (private right of action, separate consent for sharing, standalone health-privacy policy link), Nevada, Connecticut, California CPRA "sensitive PI", and the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule (unauthorised disclosure to an ad SDK counts as a breach) all apply to a non-HIPAA fitness app [17][18][19][24] (confidence: high).
- **EU-US Data Privacy Framework is valid but wobbling.** General Court upheld it (3 Sept 2025), CJEU appeal C-703/25 P pending, and after *Trump v. Slaughter* (29 June 2026) the EDPB asked the Commission to reassess. Put SCCs in every US vendor contract now, or host EU/CH data in the EU [30][31] (confidence: high).
- **App stores forbid exactly what the business model must never do:** no selling health data, no HealthKit/Health Connect data for ads or data mining, no personal health data in iCloud, a Health apps declaration in Play Console for every data type, "health-integrated games" is an approved Health Connect use case, and apps targeting children may not touch health permissions [32][36][37] (confidence: high).
- **Advertising law is where a solo German-speaking founder gets sued first.** German courts apply the *Strengeprinzip* (health claims need secured scientific evidence, often RCT-level); HWG applies the moment a claim references disease; EU Reg. 1924/2006 limits nutrition/health claims about foods; FTC demands "competent and reliable scientific evidence". The evidence-tiered design ("established / plausible / individual / experimental") is a legal asset, not just a brand [39][41][43] (confidence: high).
- **Do not write hashes of health data to a public ledger.** EDPB Guidelines 02/2025 v2.0 (7 July 2026): even keyed/salted hashes are personal data; unkeyed hashes are not acceptable confidentiality protection on a public chain; design must enable erasure. Verifiable attestations are better issued off-chain (SD-JWT/mdoc, later EUDI-Wallet-compatible EAAs) with ZK proofs computed client-side [47][48] (confidence: high).

## Findings

### 1. The medical-device boundary

**United States (FDA).** On 6 January 2026 FDA issued a revised *General Wellness: Policy for Low Risk Devices*, superseding the 2019 version [1][2][3]. Enforcement discretion applies when (i) the intended use is limited to general wellness and (ii) the product is low risk (non-invasive, not implanted, no lasers/radiation). Category 1 wellness claims make *no* reference to disease: weight management, physical fitness ("log, track, or trend exercise activity, measure aerobic fitness, ... develop or improve endurance, strength or coordination"), relaxation/stress, mental acuity, sleep management, self-esteem, sexual function; also "increase or improve muscle size or body tone" and "improve general mobility" [1]. Category 2 may reference a chronic disease only with the exact formula "may help to reduce the risk of" or "may help living well with", and only where the lifestyle link is "well understood" (named examples: heart disease, high blood pressure, type 2 diabetes) [1].

New in 2026: products using non-invasive sensing to "estimate, infer, or output physiologic parameters (e.g. blood pressure, oxygen saturation, blood glucose, heart rate variability)" may be wellness products if they are not intended for diagnosis/treatment, do not substitute for a cleared device, do not "prompt or guide specific clinical action", and do not show clinically-mimicking values unless validated. Such products "may display values, ranges, trends, baselines, or longitudinal summaries, and may contextualize these outputs in relation to sleep, activity, stress, recovery". A product is *not* wellness if its UI, labeling or marketing includes "references to specific diseases, clinical conditions, or diagnostic thresholds", alerts recommending clinical action, treatment guidance, claims of "clinical grade/accuracy", or disease-targeted intended-use statements. A gentle "a healthcare professional evaluation may be helpful" notice is tolerated only if it names no disease, does not call the value "abnormal, pathological, or diagnostic", and gives no clinical threshold [1] (confidence: high). FDA's companion Clinical Decision Support guidance (also January 2026) concerns software advising *healthcare professionals*; it is not MINMAX's path, and consumer-facing risk scores remain device territory [5] (confidence: medium). The guidance explicitly excludes dietary supplements and foods, which are regulated by other FDA centres [2].

**European Union (MDR 2017/745, MDCG 2019-11 Rev.1).** Software is a medical device if the manufacturer intends it for "diagnosis, prevention, monitoring, prediction, prognosis, treatment or alleviation of disease" (MDR Art. 2(1)) [6]. MDCG 2019-11 Rev.1 (June 2025) states plainly that "wellness or fitness apps" and other software "only intended for non-medical purposes ... do not qualify as MDSW" [6]. Risk of harm is *not* a qualification criterion; intended purpose is. Two traps for MINMAX: (a) Rev.1 added the example that "a device intended to prevent the risk of illnesses or pathologies by analysing physiological parameters (e.g. ... analysis of arterial stiffness) can be considered as a device providing information which is used to take decisions with diagnosis purpose ... class IIa" under Rule 11 [6]; (b) software that combines medical databases with patient-specific data to give "recommendations for diagnosis, prognosis, monitoring and treatment" is a device [6]. A percentile ranking of VO2max against population norms with a training suggestion is lifestyle; an "arterial age" or "metabolic risk" score is not (confidence: high). MDCG 2025-4 (June 2025) clarifies that app stores distributing a *medical-device* app become importers/distributors, which is why Apple and Google police medical claims so aggressively [7].

**Switzerland (Swissmedic).** The Medical Devices Ordinance (MedDO, SR 812.213) mirrors the MDR and Swissmedic's *Medical Device Software* information sheet v3.0 (valid from 21 April 2026) adopts MDCG 2019-11 wholesale: "the intended purpose of the medical device, as defined by the manufacturer, is definitive for qualification", independent of whether it runs in the cloud or on a phone [8]. If MINMAX ever became a device, a Swiss authorised representative (CH-REP) would be needed for EU-based manufacturers and vice versa [8] (confidence: high for qualification logic; medium for CH-REP specifics).

**United Kingdom (MHRA).** The MHRA guidance *Medical device stand-alone software including apps* (last updated July 2023) applies the UK Medical Device Regulations 2002; CE-marked devices remain accepted in Great Britain under transitional rules. The detailed wellness examples live in the PDF, which was not retrievable in this pass (unverified detail) [9] (confidence: medium).

**Allowed vs. not allowed (synthesis).** Percentiles, levels, baselines, trends and guideline-anchored targets are lifestyle; risk percentages, "abnormal", disease names, clinical thresholds and treatment targets are medical. The table in section 6 gives paired examples.

### 2. Privacy

**GDPR / EU.** Heart rate, HRV, sleep, VO2max, body composition and lab values are "data concerning health" (Art. 9). Explicit consent (Art. 9(2)(a)) is the realistic legal basis for a consumer app; legitimate interest alone does not work [10][11]. The German Datenschutzkonferenz lists mobile apps processing health data as inherently high-risk, so a DPIA (Art. 35) is mandatory before launch [10]. Data minimisation matters: raw values should stay on device or encrypted at rest, and the public profile should only expose derived claims. A DPO is mandatory under Art. 37 where core activities involve large-scale Art. 9 processing; a solo founder at MVP scale can usually argue below "large scale", but should document that assessment and revisit it at tens of thousands of EU users (confidence: medium). Age of digital consent under Art. 8 is 16 in Germany and eight other states, 13 in eight, 14/15 elsewhere [52]; MINMAX should set 16+ in the EU and verify age at onboarding rather than run per-country logic at MVP.

**EU AI Act.** A rules-plus-ML engine that turns metrics into weekly goals is "minimal risk" and unregulated [12]. Three live obligations: Art. 50 transparency for any chatbot/coach persona (applied 2 Aug 2026) [14]; Art. 5 prohibitions on manipulative or vulnerability-exploiting techniques, which is a design constraint for XP loops and streak pressure [12]; and Art. 4 AI literacy. Annex III lists "health/life insurance risk and pricing" and eligibility for essential services as high-risk; if MINMAX attestations are later consumed by insurers, the insurer is the high-risk deployer, but MINMAX would need to decide whether it becomes a provider of a component [12]. Digital Omnibus Reg. (EU) 2026/1744 (in force late July 2026) pushed Annex III obligations to 2 Dec 2027 and Annex I to 2 Aug 2028, and created a "small mid-cap" relief tier [13] (confidence: high).

**Switzerland (revised FADP, in force 1 Sept 2023).** Health data is "sensitive personal data"; express consent is needed for sensitive data and for high-risk profiling by private persons; DPIA for high-risk processing (consult a data protection adviser instead of the FDPIC is permitted); no DPO obligation for private companies; processing register exemption under 250 employees for low-risk activities; fines up to CHF 250,000 against the responsible *individual*, which matters for a solo founder [15]. The Swiss-US DPF applies since 15 Sept 2024 [16] (confidence: high).

**United Kingdom.** UK GDPR treats health as special category data with the same explicit-consent and DPIA logic [11]. The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (Royal Assent 17 June 2025) relaxes research re-use and "broad consent", relevant if MINMAX later runs cohort studies [53] (confidence: medium).

**United States.** HIPAA does not apply: an app a consumer downloads independently is neither a covered entity nor a business associate, even when data originates from a clinic [22][23]. It would apply only if MINMAX were engaged by a provider/insurer to handle PHI on their behalf [22]. Instead: the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule (effective 29 July 2024) treats a fitness app syncing device data as a "PHR vendor" and defines unauthorised disclosure, including to ad platforms, as a breach [24]. Washington's My Health My Data Act covers any entity targeting Washington consumers; "consumer health data" expressly includes "bodily functions, vital signs, symptoms, or measurements", biometric data and inferences derived by algorithms [17]; it requires opt-in consent for collection, *separate* consent for sharing, a signed "valid authorization" for any sale, a standalone health-privacy policy linked from the homepage, deletion including backups, and every violation is a per-se Consumer Protection Act violation with a private right of action [17][18]. Nevada SB 370 (effective 31 March 2024) is similar but narrower; Connecticut's SB 3 covers "consumer health data" within its general privacy law [19]. New York's NYHIPA was vetoed in December 2025 and reintroduced in 2026 (A.10357/S.9269) [20]. California's CPRA treats health as "sensitive personal information" with a right to limit use; regulations effective 1 Jan 2026 require risk assessments when inferring health characteristics with automated processing [21] (confidence: high).

**Rest of world.** Brazil LGPD: health is sensitive data requiring "specific and highlighted consent"; extraterritorial for services offered to Brazilian residents; 3-business-day breach notice; DPO required unless a small agent (ANPD exemption), fines up to 2%/BRL 50m [25]. India DPDP Rules notified 14 Nov 2025; core duties bite around May 2027; verifiable parental consent for all under-18s; breach notice to the Board within 72 hours; health is not a separate category [26]. Japan APPI: "medical history" is special-care-required information requiring prior consent; cross-border transfers need informed consent about the destination country; extraterritorial for services to Japanese residents; a 2026 amendment introducing administrative fines is still in draft [27]. Australia: small-business exemption (AUD 3m) still stands but a statutory privacy tort applies to everyone since 10 June 2025; a Children's Online Privacy Code is in draft; a second reform tranche closed consultation 18 Sept 2026 [28]. China PIPL: transferring sensitive data of >10,000 individuals/year triggers a CAC security assessment; separate consent and data localisation duties make a China launch impractical for a solo founder, so geoblocking China (and the US DOJ-restricted countries) at MVP is the rational default [29] (confidence: medium).

**Cross-border transfers.** The EU-US DPF survived *Latombe* at first instance (T-553/23, 3 Sept 2025); the appeal C-703/25 P is pending with no hearing date [30][54]. On 29 June 2026 the US Supreme Court in *Trump v. Slaughter* removed FTC commissioners' for-cause protection; on 31 July 2026 the EDPB formally asked the Commission to assess the impact; nobody has told companies to stop relying on the DPF, but SCCs plus transfer impact assessments are the standard hedge [31]. Practical consequence for MINMAX: host EU/UK/CH user data in an EU region, choose processors (analytics, crash reporting, email) that offer EU hosting and SCCs, and avoid US ad SDKs entirely.

### 3. App store rules

**Apple.** Guideline 5.1.3(i): apps "may not use or disclose to third parties data gathered in the health, fitness, and medical research context ... for advertising, marketing, or other use-based data mining purposes other than improving health management, or for the purpose of health research, and then only with permission"; a benefit such as a reduced insurance premium is allowed only if the app is submitted by the entity providing the benefit and data is not shared with a third party; the specific health data collected must be disclosed. 5.1.3(ii): no false data written to HealthKit and no "personal health information in iCloud". 5.1.3(iii)-(iv): human-subject research needs informed consent (parental for minors) and an ethics board [32]. 1.4.1: apps claiming to measure blood pressure, glucose or SpO2 "using only the sensors on the device are not permitted", accuracy methodology must be disclosed, and apps "should remind users to check with a doctor" [32]. 5.1.4: apps for kids may not include third-party analytics/advertising [32]. HealthKit usage-description strings and the HealthKit capability are required in the binary (standard practice; Apple's HealthKit documentation page could not be parsed in this pass, so treat specifics as unverified). Age ratings were overhauled on 24 July 2025 to 4+/9+/13+/16+/18+ with new mandatory questions on "medical or wellness topics" and the option to set a higher minimum age; answers were due by 31 Jan 2026 [33]. Subscriptions (3.1.2): title, length, price and functional privacy-policy/EULA links must appear in-app; free trials run one week to three months [34].

**Google Play.** The Health Content and Services policy covers any app with health features or health data access; non-device apps must carry a disclaimer that they do not "diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any medical condition", must not be "misleading or potentially harmful", and medical-device apps must show regulatory approval on request [35]. Health Connect data is "personal and sensitive user data"; approved use cases are "fitness and wellness, rewards, fitness coaching, corporate wellness, medical care, health research, and games" [36]; "health-integrated games: gameplay influenced by real-world activity data" is explicitly listed [37]. Prohibited: transfer or sale to ad platforms/data brokers, use for credit, insurance or employment determinations, headless apps, and connecting health permissions to anything that "solely target[s] children" [36][37]. Every Health Connect data type must be justified in the Health apps declaration (App content page); access is allow-listed per package name and reviewed; the privacy policy must be a public, non-geofenced, non-PDF URL matching the one shown inside Health Connect [38][35]. Google Fit APIs end in 2026, so Health Connect is the only Android path [38] (confidence: high).

### 4. Consumer protection on health claims

**FTC (US).** The December 2022 *Health Products Compliance Guidance* explicitly covers "health-related apps" and requires "competent and reliable scientific evidence"; for health benefit claims RCTs are "generally the type of substantiation that experts would require"; implied claims and the "net impression" of charts and imagery count; disclaimers and DSHEA-style fine print do not cure a deceptive claim; testimonials must reflect typical results [39]. MINMAX's "Top 0.1%"-style competitor targets would fail this test.

**EU UCPD.** Annex I point 17 blacklists "falsely claiming that a product is able to cure illnesses, dysfunction or malformations" [40]. Reg. (EC) 1924/2006 governs nutrition and health claims about *foods* in commercial communications; only authorised claims may be used, e.g. "source of protein" requires that at least 12% of energy comes from protein [41]. If MINMAX recommends products (protein powder, creatine), its wording about those products inherits these rules.

**Germany (HWG/UWG).** The HWG applies to advertising for medicines, medical devices and "procedures, treatments and items with disease reference"; § 3 bans misleading efficacy claims; § 11 restricts testimonials, fear-mongering and before/after imagery toward consumers; § 7 limits freebies (a freemium medical app can be a gift-in-advertising problem) [42]. A wellness app with no disease reference stays outside the HWG, but § 5 UWG still applies with the *Strengeprinzip*: health-related claims must rest on "gesicherte wissenschaftliche Erkenntnis", courts often expect randomised placebo-controlled studies, and the burden of proof shifts to the advertiser once a claim is shown to be scientifically disputed; violations trigger Abmahnungen with cost reimbursement [43] (confidence: high).

**Switzerland.** Art. 69 MedDO: device advertising may only contain statements matching the product information and misleading claims about intended use, safety or performance are prohibited [45]. For foods/supplements, LIV Annexes 13/14 list the only permitted nutrition and health claims and "all statements suggesting properties for prevention, treatment or cure of disease are prohibited" (Heilanpreisungsverbot), enforced by cantonal authorities [44]. The UWG (Art. 3 lit. b) covers misleading statements generally, and the Lauterkeitskommission is self-regulatory without fining power.

**Supplement recommendations and liability.** The revised EU Product Liability Directive 2024/2853 (transposition by 9 Dec 2026) brings stand-alone software and apps under strict liability for personal injury and covers post-market control via updates [46]. Recommending a dose of melatonin or NAC "before training" is the kind of personalised, non-guideline advice that creates both advertising-law exposure and tort exposure; MINMAX should restrict supplements to the "individual/experimental" tier with explicit non-advice framing and never specify therapeutic doses (confidence: medium).

### 5. Zero-knowledge / verifiable-credential layer

**GDPR and public ledgers.** EDPB Guidelines 02/2025 v2.0 (adopted 7 July 2026) state: "In general, it is not advisable to store personal data on the blockchain"; a salted or keyed hash of personal data stored on-chain "will also be considered personal data"; "unsalted or unkeyed hashes should, in general, not be considered sufficient to guarantee the necessary level of confidentiality protection ... on a public blockchain"; public chains "should only be employed if public access ... is necessary"; erasure must be designed in up front (deleting the off-chain key/salt or witness so the on-chain commitment becomes "useless"), and a DPIA is required. ZKPs and other PETs are welcomed but "should be carefully tested, validated" [47]. Storing a *hiding* cryptographic commitment with the witness held off-chain is the one on-chain pattern the EDPB describes as workable [47] (confidence: high).

**eIDAS 2.0 / EUDI Wallet.** Reg. (EU) 2024/1183 obliges each member state to offer a certified EUDI Wallet by the end of 2026; electronic attestations of attributes (EAAs) can be issued by private parties as non-qualified EAAs and carried in the wallet with selective disclosure as a "basic design feature" [49][48]. The Architecture Reference Framework discusses BBS+/BBS# and zk-SNARK-based proofs for unlinkability but says ZKP support is "expected to be introduced following the launch", and researchers have criticised SD-JWT's lack of unlinkability [48]. Health-sector relying parties will have to accept the wallet within 36 months of implementing acts [49] (confidence: medium). The European Health Data Space Regulation (EU) 2025/327 (applies from 26 March 2027) defines "wellness application" and creates a labelling scheme and registry only for wellness apps that *claim interoperability with EHR systems*; apps that make no such claim have no EHDS duties [50][51] (confidence: medium).

Implication: a MINMAX "VO2max >= 50 (Garmin-derived)" attestation should be a signed, selectively-disclosable credential (SD-JWT VC or mdoc today, EUDI-compatible EAA later) issued by MINMAX's server; a ZK proof of a threshold is computed on the user's device for the verifier; if any ledger is used at all, it holds only a hiding commitment or revocation accumulator, never a hash of the raw value. Midnight or any other chain is therefore an optional anchoring layer, not the data store.

### 6. Checklist and phrasings

**MVP must-haves (before public launch)**
1. Written intended-purpose statement ("general fitness, nutrition habits, mobility and recovery tracking for healthy adults; not for diagnosis, prevention, monitoring or treatment of any disease") and a claims review gate for every UI string, store listing and marketing asset [1][6][8].
2. Age gate 16+ (EU) / 18+ where simpler; no Kids Category; no health permissions in anything child-targeted [33][37][52].
3. GDPR/FADP/UK: Art. 9 explicit consent flow separate from T&Cs; DPIA completed and filed; records of processing; EU-hosted infrastructure; SCCs with every US processor; data deletion that purges backups within a defined window; privacy policy on a public non-PDF URL identical in-app, in Play Console and in Health Connect [10][15][35][38].
4. US: standalone "Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy" link on the website homepage; opt-in collection consent and separate sharing consent (Washington model applied globally); no sale of health data; incident-response plan meeting FTC HBNR timelines [17][18][24].
5. Stores: Apple HealthKit usage strings, no PHI in iCloud, disclose exact data types; Play Health apps declaration with justification per data type; Google-mandated "does not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent" disclaimer; subscription screen with title/length/price and policy links [32][34][35][38].
6. No third-party advertising or analytics SDKs that receive health data; crash/analytics tools configured to exclude health fields [32][36].
7. Evidence tiers shown on every recommendation with citations; supplements only in "individual/experimental" tier, no therapeutic doses, no disease references; FTC-grade substantiation file for any marketing claim [39][43].
8. AI coach labelled as AI (Art. 50) and gamification reviewed against Art. 5 manipulation criteria [12][14].
9. Terms with a Swiss/German-law-compliant disclaimer, plus a short "talk to a doctor before changing training if you have a medical condition" notice (Apple 1.4.1 expectation) [32].

**Later (post-MVP / scale)**
- DPO appointment when EU Art. 9 processing becomes "large scale"; Brazil DPO when leaving the small-agent exemption; India DPDP consent manager integration before May 2027; Japan transfer disclosures when localising there [25][26][27].
- Verifiable-credential issuer (SD-JWT VC/mdoc) with device-side ZK threshold proofs; EUDI Wallet EAA alignment when wallets ship; separate DPIA for any ledger anchoring per EDPB 02/2025 [47][48].
- Insurer/employer partnerships: legal review for AI Act Annex III exposure and Apple 5.1.3 "benefit" carve-out (only the benefit provider may submit the app) [12][32].
- EHDS wellness-app label only if interoperability with EHR systems is ever claimed [50].
- Geoblocks on China and DOJ-restricted countries; revisit when resources allow [29].

## Implications for MINMAX

- **Build the claims gate into the product, not the legal review.** Every stat, quest and region description is generated from templates; put the forbidden-term list and the evidence-tier label into the content pipeline so copy cannot ship with "risk", "abnormal", "prevent", "treat", "diagnose" or named diseases.
- **Percentiles and levels are the regulatory sweet spot.** Population percentiles, personal baselines, trends and guideline-anchored targets are exactly what FDA's 2026 text allows ("values, ranges, trends, baselines, or longitudinal summaries"). Risk scores are the one feature class to refuse permanently unless MINMAX becomes a device company.
- **Lab results are the riskiest input.** Accept them as provenance-tagged facts, display them with the lab's own reference ranges, and never compute targets or interpretations; route "what should I do about this" to a clinician.
- **Separate the game economy from health-data sharing.** Guild/challenge features must consume attestations (level, threshold claim), never raw values; this satisfies Apple 5.1.3, Health Connect rules and Washington's separate-sharing-consent requirement in one design move.
- **Architecture: EU-first hosting with SCC-backed vendors.** This neutralises most DPF uncertainty and is also what Swiss and UK users expect.
- **Monetise via subscription, never via data; review gamification for Art. 5 manipulation.** Store policies and US state laws converge on the first point; a B2B attestation API for insurers is only viable with the insurer as the regulated deployer and the user as the sole data holder.
- **The evidence-tier taxonomy doubles as the substantiation file.** Every "established" claim should link to a guideline or meta-analysis; this is what an Abmahnung or FTC inquiry will ask for.
